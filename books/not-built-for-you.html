Not Built for You

Why Standard Money Advice Fails Women, and the Math That Actually Builds Security

Standard personal finance advice encodes a hidden assumption: continuous income, no wage gap, no negotiation penalty, a full career with no break in it. Save fifteen percent, build six months of reserves, invest early. Every rule is mathematically correct for that imaginary person and miscalibrated for almost everyone reading it. This book does not try to fix a reader's discipline. It fixes the instrument the advice was built on.

The Lifetime Ledger recalculates what a career break, a lower starting salary, or years of part time work actually cost across a working life, not just this year's paycheck, and shows why a percentage based savings rule quietly compounds a gap instead of closing it. The Income Firewall separates what a reader can protect from claims on her earnings, structural and personal, so that money set aside for security stops being the first thing renegotiated under pressure. The Earnings Engine addresses the negotiation penalty directly: the specific, documented cost of asking for less because asking for more has previously been read as difficult, and gives a method for closing that gap that does not depend on changing how negotiation feels.

The Claiming Calendar turns retirement and benefit timing into a concrete schedule rather than a vague someday, because timing decisions made without a plan default to whatever is easiest in the moment, and that default is rarely the one that pays the most over decades. Each framework in this book is delivered as a corrected calculation with the reader's actual numbers, not a motivational reframe of the same generic advice.

The book is honest that none of this removes the structural conditions it describes. The wage gap, the caregiving penalty, and the negotiation tax are real and are not solved by better math alone. What the math changes is whether a reader is planning against her actual numbers or against a version of her life that was never true, which is the difference between a plan that holds under pressure and one that quietly fails the first time life does not follow the template it was built on.

Common Questions

Who is this book for?

Women who have followed standard budgeting and savings advice and still feel behind, and who suspect the problem was never their discipline.

What makes this book different?

It names the specific hidden assumptions in standard financial advice, the wage gap, the career break penalty, the negotiation tax, and recalculates each recommendation against a reader's actual numbers instead of a generic model.

What will I learn?

How a career break or lower starting salary compounds across a working life, how to protect income from competing claims, and how to build a concrete claiming and negotiation timeline instead of relying on someday.
